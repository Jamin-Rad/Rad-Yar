# Shoulder anatomy: authoring plan and evidence

Scope: new `/msk/schulter/anatomie` lesson and a single curriculum entry under Shoulder. Requested publication is an in-progress teaching lesson, not scientific acceptance. No existing access gates or completion allowlists change.

Audience: beginners. Source language: Persian; German and English preserve the same teaching units.

Observable goals: identify the three bones; distinguish acromion from coracoid; locate the glenoid and scapular neck; identify greater/lesser tuberosities and intertubercular groove; distinguish anatomical from surgical neck as spatial landmarks; recognise that projection and slice level change visibility.

## Outline and visual plan

- Orientation: approved first-step illustration, three bones, head–glenoid relationship, transfer to real Grashey and coronal PD MRI.
- Landmarks: acromion/AC joint → coracoid → glenoid/scapular neck → tuberosities/groove → humeral necks. Each microstep has anatomical reconstruction, appropriate radiograph and MRI, independent labels and explanatory feedback. Only teach targets visible in the selected frame; limitations are visible in captions.
- Views: AP external/internal rotation, scapular Y and axillary projections; axial, coronal oblique and sagittal oblique MRI. No numeric diagnostic thresholds or pathological diagnoses taught.
- Retrieval: image identification with explanatory feedback. No external quiz/flashcard links until a corresponding bank is authored.
- Summary: four portable orientation rules.
