### Open source conflict: hepatic AVM, `SRC-AVM-01`

Abdomen.pdf p. 11 was rendered at high resolution and visually checked. It labels the subject “Arteriovenöse Malformation (AVM)”, defines a direct artery–vein communication, and shows multiple CT/MR frames. Its text says normal DWI and early arterial contrast enhancement followed by wash-out; the differential note warns about HCC and recommends considering cirrhosis/DWI. The actual frames have no visible phase labels, arrows, caption or case citation identifying a draining vessel or the exact enhancing structure.

The existing `/abdomen/leber/avm` lesson is linked only to two Radiopaedia case pages (hepatic AVM and HHT liver); it has no primary-literature citation. Its CT/MRI tables teach early venous filling and transient regional enhancement, then state rapid equalisation/no relevant persistent wash-out. Those statements may describe a shunt/perfusion phenomenon, whereas p. 11 may describe a discrete hypervascular lesion associated with a vascular malformation. The page image does not establish which interpretation applies.

The current EASL vascular-liver guideline (published online 2025; Journal of Hepatology 2026) distinguishes early portal-tree enhancement in arterioportal fistula from early hepatic-vein enhancement in hepatic arteriovenous fistula, supports Doppler and contrast CT as useful complementary tests, and recommends assessing associated tumour, chronic liver disease, trauma/intervention and HHT context. It does not identify the specific lesion shown on Abdomen.pdf p. 11 or state that all AVM-associated focal enhancement must lack wash-out. See [EASL guideline page](https://easl.eu/publication-category/clinical-practice-guidelines/) and [full guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2025.08.001).

**Disposition after revision:** the route no longer generalizes “no relevant wash-out.” It now records the PDF's normal-DWI and early-enhancement/followed-by-wash-out statements as case-specific observations, embeds the user-authorized source page with a limitation caption, and includes a localized retrieval check. The uncertainty is still scientifically unresolved: source citation/case identity, phase labels and exact lesion/draining-vessel correspondence are unavailable. The broader shunt/HHT material is supplemental. See [AVM source map](avm-source-map.md) and `public/educational/avm-liver/source-provenance.json`. Do not equate wash-out alone with HCC or AVM; Dr. Zia review and browser verification remain pending.

Abdomen PDF page 5 was visually inspected for simple liver cyst findings; pages 14–15 for FNH; 16–18 for adenoma and FNH/adenoma comparison; 19–23 for HCC and LI-RADS v2018; 24–25 for hypo-/hypervascular metastases; and 26–27 for diffuse/focal steatosis. Pages 40–42 were visually inspected: page 40 shows pseudocyst definition/context and differential; page 41 shows IPMN definition, types, duct communication and CT/MRI/MRCP examples; page 42 shows an older Fukuoka-era risk list and management note. The new cyst lesson explicitly labels that management note as dated and uses Kyoto 2024 for the four current high-risk stigmata. Thorax PDF pages 17 and 49 were visually inspected; page 17 supports the newly added hydrostatic oedema pattern and associated findings. Page 49 supports a cardiogenic/non-cardiogenic comparison, but the lesson adds the morphology cautiously and labels it context-dependent.

Primary source consulted for the updated IPMN high-risk stigmata: Furukawa et al., *International evidence-based Kyoto guidelines for the management of intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm of the pancreas*, Pancreatology 2024, [doi:10.1016/j.pan.2023.12.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pan.2023.12.009). For hepatic AML variability, pathology-correlated primary cohorts include Yang et al. 2009 [doi:10.3748/wjg.15.3417](https://doi.org/10.3748/wjg.15.3417) and Tsai et al. 2017 [PubMed 28809731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28809731/). These small retrospective studies demonstrate overlap, not a stand-alone diagnostic rule. These source checks do not constitute final clinical approval.
