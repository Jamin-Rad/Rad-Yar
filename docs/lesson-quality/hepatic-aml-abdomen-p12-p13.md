## Source verification and limits

- Wang et al. 2009 is a retrospective, pathology-correlated dynamic CT series of 10 patients. It reports persistent enhancement in non-fat components of sporadic AML and early enhancement with rapid washout in seven fat-deficient nodules across two patients with TSC. These data establish that washout can occur, not a prevalence rule.
- The route's former “Tsai et al. 2017” citation pointed to PubMed PMID 28809731, whose actual authors are Ding et al. The route label and evidence text are corrected. Ding et al. compared six pathology-confirmed HAML with 33 noncirrhotic HCC; all five hypervascular HAML were misdiagnosed as HCC by CT/MRI. The cohort supports overlap, but is small and retrospective.
- A gadoxetic MRI comparison included 12 AML and 27 HCC: portal washout occurred in both, and HBP hypointensity was more frequent in AML but also present in HCC. A separate pathology-confirmed case report documents atypical HBP hyperintensity associated with entrapped hepatocytes. Another pathology-proven cohort (18 AML, 36 HCC) documented early draining veins in 27.8% and 2.8%, respectively. The page therefore treats early drainage as a clue, not a frequent or exclusive sign; usual HBP hypointensity is non-specific and rare exceptions remain possible.
- The PDF's “pathognomonic” −20 HU statement is not retained. The page uses macroscopic fat as a clue and explicitly preserves fat-poor HAML and fat-containing HCC overlap.
- Source pages contain no diagnostic case images. The existing hero diagram is a conceptual schematic only. Browser UX review and Dr. Zia's scientific approval remain open.
