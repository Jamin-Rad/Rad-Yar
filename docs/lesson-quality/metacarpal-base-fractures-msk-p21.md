# Metacarpal base and neck fractures — MSK.pdf pp.21, 24

## Scope and learning path

- Curriculum ID: `metakarpale-frakturen`; route: `/msk/trauma/metakarpale-frakturen`.
- Audience: radiology learners reviewing hand trauma morphology.
- Learning outcome: distinguish Bennett, Rolando and Winterstein patterns by articular extension and fragment configuration; recognize the usual fifth-metacarpal neck boxer fracture and orient its angulation across radiographic views.
- Chapter outline: (1) joint surface and fragment count; (2) side-by-side pattern comparison with the supplied PDF illustration; (3) base-fracture retrieval decision; (4) fifth-metacarpal neck morphology, radiographic views and a second retrieval decision; (5) concise take-home rules.

The first is an illustration, not a diagnostic case. The p.24 montage is a supplied teaching image whose page does not state a patient/case contributor.

## Boxer fracture extension — MSK.pdf p.24

- Scope: physical PDF p.24, printed p.23, heading “OS metacarpale V, subkapital – Boxer-Fraktur”. The full rendered p.24 and two radiographs were visually inspected; p.23 and p.25 were also checked as boundaries. The source gives a fist strike against a hard object, subcapital fracture of V (rarely IV), and palmar tilt of the metacarpal head. It contains no treatment threshold or contemporary treatment recommendation used here.
- Learning path: identify neck location and mechanism; distinguish palmar head tilt from a dorsal apex angle; inspect a three-view hand examination (PA/AP, lateral, oblique), keeping projection/positioning limits in mind; answer a localized retrieval question.

- External verification: Carter et al., *Metacarpal Neck Fractures: A Review of Surgical Indications and Techniques*, 2016, [PMC5078834](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078834/), supports the clenched-fist mechanism, neck location, dorsal-apex configuration and PA/lateral/oblique projections.
