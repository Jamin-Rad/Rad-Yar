## Evidence

- User-provided source: `Abdomen.pdf`, physical p.72 (printed p.71), visually
  inspected; text identifies bowel-wall thickening, reduced enhancement,
  pneumatosis intestinalis and portal venous gas.
- Millet I et al. *Value of CT findings to predict surgical ischemia in small
  bowel obstruction: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Eur Radiol.
  2015;25:1823–1835. [PMID 25850889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25850889/).
  Reduced mural enhancement was the most specific evaluated sign; bowel-wall
  thickness was less discriminative.
- Le Corre P et al. *Diagnostic Performance of Individual CT Signs for
  Identifying Ischemia and Necrosis in Small Bowel Obstruction: A Systematic
  Review and Meta-Analysis.* AJR. 2026. [PMID 42053388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42053388/).
  The meta-analysis found no sign with sufficient negative likelihood ratio to
  reliably exclude ischaemia or necrosis.
- ACR Appropriateness Criteria, *Suspected Small-Bowel Obstruction* (2019):
  [narrative](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69476/Narrative/), listing decreased
  or increased bowel-wall enhancement, thickening, edema/fluid, pneumatosis and
  venous gas among signs that suggest ischaemia.
