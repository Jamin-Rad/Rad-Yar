## Validation and open gates

- Current primary adrenal-incidentaloma reference: ESE/ENSAT Clinical Practice Guideline 2023, [official guideline directory](https://www.ese-hormones.org/publications/directory/ese-clinical-practice-guideline-on-the-management-of-adrenal-incidentalomas-in-collaboration-with-the-european-network-for-the-study-of-adrenal-tumors/) and [full guideline](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article/189/1/G1/7198474?login=false). Recommendation R.2.3: homogeneous lesions with unenhanced CT ≤10 HU are benign and need no additional imaging independent of size. Recommendation R.2.5: ≥4 cm with heterogeneity or HU >20 warrants expert-team discussion; other imaging profiles require individualized assessment (R.2.4/R.2.6). Hormonal function remains a separate assessment. This bounded review does not add the full imaging or endocrine pathway.
- ESE/ENSAT 2023 washout evidence: [systematic review, washout section and Table 4](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article/189/1/G1/7198474?login=false). Common >60% absolute / >40% relative cutoffs have very-low evidence in true incidentalomas; 22% / 8% of malignancies, respectively, were not correctly identified in one study. Relative >58% is listed with very-low-strength evidence from one 253-tumor study and specificity 15%, with external-validation caution. Nonhomogeneous lesions should not be characterized using MRI/washout CT. The guideline states the optimal delay is uncertain, at least 10 minutes is needed, and 15 minutes may be preferable.
- ESE/ENSAT 2023 recommends plasma-free or urinary-fractionated metanephrines to exclude pheochromocytoma when an adrenal lesion is not typical for a benign adenoma (R.3.9); [primary guideline](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article/189/1/G1/7198474?login=false). The Endocrine Society PPGL guideline recommends initial diagnosis with plasma-free or urinary-fractionated metanephrines and advises starting localization imaging after clear biochemical evidence; [primary guideline](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/99/6/1915/2537399). The lesson therefore treats enhancement/washout as imaging observations, not biochemical confirmation or exclusion.
- Neuroblastoma imaging source check (2026-10-22): the COG Diagnostic Imaging Committee / Society for Pediatric Radiology Oncology Committee white paper describes neuroblastoma along the sympathetic chain, commonly abdominal/adrenal, and recommends anatomic CT/MRI to evaluate the primary lesion and image-defined anatomic relationships; [Lai et al., Pediatr Blood Cancer 2023, DOI 10.1002/pbc.29974](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pbc.29974). The INRG task-force consensus defines image-defined risk factors from tumor relationships to vessels, kidney and spinal canal; it is staging guidance, but the lesson does not assign a stage or treatment category; [Brisse et al., Radiology 2011, DOI 10.1148/radiol.11101352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21908736/). The source-page lung-versus-bone contrast remains excluded from learner teaching and assessment.
- Myelolipoma imaging review: a clinical-radiology review describes histologic admixture of mature adipose and haematopoietic tissue, wide variation in their proportions, fat-dominant and myeloid-dominant patterns, and enhancement of the myeloid component; occasional calcifications are described without needing a broad percentage for this lesson. [Calissendorff et al., Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. 2021;9:767–775, DOI 10.1016/S2213-8587(21)00178-9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34450092/). For MRI terminology, the Society of Abdominal Radiology consensus defines macroscopic fat by intratumoral signal loss on fat-suppressed imaging and/or India-ink artifact at fat–water interfaces; it distinguishes this from diffuse microscopic intracellular fat signal drop. The p62 text comparison is unchanged in this p61 audit. [Schieda et al., J Magn Reson Imaging 2019;49:917–926, DOI 10.1002/jmri.26542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30693607/).
- Pathology-correlated washout evidence: a multicenter retrospective cohort of 548 histologically confirmed pheochromocytomas found attenuation data for 376 tumors, with 374 >10 HU and two exactly 10 HU; among 76 tumors with washout data, 22 (28.9%) met high APW and/or RPW criteria. This selected cohort supports that neither a standalone HU nor washout rule should be taught as absolute; it is not a general prevalence estimate. [Eisenhofer et al., JCEM 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30383267/).
