## Scientific qualification

The classical three-narrowing description is retained as anatomy because that is what the source figure teaches. It is not presented as proof that calculi commonly lodge at every site. Kamo M, et al. “There are no three physiological narrowings in the upper urinary tract: a new concept of the retroperitoneal anatomy around the ureter.” *Jpn J Radiol.* 2021;39:407–413. doi:[10.1007/s11604-020-01080-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11604-020-01080-7). The review distinguishes the iliac-vessel crossing from the nearby upper-ureter “crossing point” associated with stone location. The primary CT cohort by Eisner BH, et al. found 1.1% of stones at the iliac crossing at emergency presentation: *J Urol.* 2009;182:165–168. doi:[10.1016/j.juro.2009.02.131](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2009.02.131). This source review is not final clinical approval.

Perirenal stranding remains a nonspecific imaging observation. The age-related source wording is not taught as a stand-alone rule. No disease thresholds, management steps or additional diagnostic claims were added.
