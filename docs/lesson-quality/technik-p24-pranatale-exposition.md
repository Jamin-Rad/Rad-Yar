## Current source verification (accessed 2026-10-19)

- **Current law:** [StrlSchV official consolidated text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/strlschv_2018/BJNR203600018.html), §1(22) defines a `Vorkommnis` around an event causing or potentially causing unintended exposure. Under §108(1–2), the Strahlenschutzverantwortlicher ensures that a significant event is reported to the competent authority without delay, with all available information required for its assessment; §108(3) requires supplementary information after the §109(1) investigation. §109(1–2) requires prompt systematic investigation of causes/effects and prompt recording. Event significance is assessed under Annexes 14/15. §120(1–2) concerns pregnancy inquiry, urgency and exposure reduction before a medically necessary application; it is not conflated with post-exposure reporting. The lesson gives no determination of a particular case.
- **Current national recommendation:** [Strahlenschutzkommission, “Effekte nach pränataler Strahlenexposition”](https://ssk.de/publikationen/2025-10-28-praenatale-exposition), adopted 27–28 Oct 2025, published 15 May 2026. It recommends careful benefit-risk assessment for radiodiagnostic/therapeutic measures and an interdisciplinary consultation to prepare discussion except in emergencies. The lesson uses this for communication/process, not to derive a case-specific risk.
- **Dose-assessment method:** [DGMP/DRG joint report, 2019 revision](https://www.apt2.drg.de/de-DE/8860/bericht-praenatale-strahlenexposition-aus-medizinischer-indikation/) is hosted by DRG; full text link on that official page was checked. Fiebich et al., [“Prenatal radiation exposure in diagnostic and interventional radiology”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33327031/), Rofo 2021, DOI 10.1055/a-1313-7527, reports a professional two-step, case-specific assessment approach and that CT/interventional cases may need more detailed calculation. No numeric bands or patient-specific management conclusions imported.
- **Event-handling roles:** [BfS guide (2020), “Umgang mit Vorkommnissen in Röntgendiagnostik und Nuklearmedizin”](https://doris.bfs.de/jspui/bitstream/urn%3Anbn%3Ade%3A0221-2021010624674/3/2020_BfS_3617S42332_3617S42333_LF.pdf), pp.11, 15 and process examples: identifies unintended uterine direct-beam exposure as a recognition criterion and describes involving MPE and radiation-protection leads. It is operational guidance, not the legal authority; current law governs legal classification.

## Teaching path, assessment and limits

1. Reconstruct pregnancy timing, body region and beam/exposure path; promptly assess event significance with the responsible Strahlenschutzverantwortlicher against current criteria.
2. If the event is significant, the responsible person ensures notification without delay with available information. Do not wait for full dosimetry or investigation; supplement the notification after investigation.
3. In parallel, investigate and record causes/effects under §109 and obtain individual MPE dosimetry; then prepare the patient discussion with the treating physician and appropriate interdisciplinary support.
4. Embedded self-check asks what to do after abdominal CT in a patient later found pregnant. Correct response: promptly assess significance with the responsible person; if significant, notify without delay with available information while investigation and MPE dosimetry proceed, then prepare an individual discussion. Do not calculate fetal dose from DLP alone or make automatic reportability or pregnancy-outcome decisions. This is embedded only; no global MCQ-bank entry is claimed.
