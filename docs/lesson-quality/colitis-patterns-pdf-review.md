# Colitis patterns — Abdomen.pdf pp.81–82

Date: 2026-10-19. Scope: new route `/abdomen/gi/kolitis-patterns`, curriculum IDs `pmc-kolitis` and `colitis-ulcerosa`. This is a focused CT-pattern lesson, not a comprehensive IBD or infection-management chapter.

## Learning path and visual plan

1. Separate acute infectious from chronic inflammatory pattern.
2. PMC: wall thickening, accordion and target patterns; distribution caveat.
3. UC: continuous rectum-first distribution and chronic remodelling; complication warning.
4. Compare PMC with UC and distinguish a pattern from confirmation.
5. Self-check tests that segmental disease and layered wall findings are not diagnostic proof.
6. Four portable CT rules.

Visuals: a code-native schematic contrasts discontinuous PMC segments (orange) and continuous UC extent (teal), with a localized color key; one source CT panel shows the source-labelled PMC accordion pattern; one source coronal CT panel from the UC slide is captioned with the caveat that a single view cannot prove full disease distribution. Full-page renders and crops were inspected. The PDF embeds rID 22935 and rID 16054 at tiny font sizes; these are recorded as embedded identifiers only and do not identify a verified case page, website or contributor. ## External support

- Fishman EK et al. *Pseudomembranous colitis: CT evaluation of 26 cases*. Radiology. 1991;180:57–60. DOI 10.1148/radiology.180.1.2052723. Primary CT series supports variable thickness and segmental as well as extensive involvement; does not make CT specific.
- Srisajjakul S et al. *Multidetector computed tomography features of positive endoscopic or toxin assay Clostridium difficile colitis*. J Med Assoc Thai. 2013;96:477–484. PMID 23691703. Retrospective, small toxin-/endoscopy-confirmed cohort; used as corroboration of distribution/sign variability only.
- ECCO–ESGAR–ESP–IBUS. *Guideline on Diagnostics and Monitoring of IBD, Part 1*. J Crohns Colitis. 2025;19(7):jjaf106. DOI 10.1093/ecco-jcc/jjaf106. Supports multimodal initial IBD diagnosis and typical continuous rectum-first UC mucosal pattern.
- IDSA/SHEA. *Clinical Practice Guidelines for C. difficile Infection: 2017 Update*. Clin Infect Dis. 2018;66(7):e1–e48. DOI 10.1093/cid/cix1085. Supports clinical and stool-test context; lesson does not cover treatment.
