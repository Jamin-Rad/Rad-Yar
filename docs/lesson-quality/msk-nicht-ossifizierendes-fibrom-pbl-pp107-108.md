# NOF / FCD lesson source ledger

**Route:** `/msk/tumoren/nicht-ossifizierendes-fibrom`  
**Source:** User-provided `MSK.pdf`, PDF pp. 107–108 (printed pp. 106–107). Both pages were inspected as rendered images; p. 108's two radiographic panels were cropped and visually checked.  
**Audience / promise:** Radiology learners; recognize a typical NOF/FCD on radiographs, distinguish the conventional size/location labels, and know when uncertainty or fracture concern warrants further assessment.  
**Language source:** German PDF; content authored and semantically compared in German, English and Persian.

## Teaching path and visual plan

1. FCD–NOF spectrum and conventional distinction (FCD <3 cm/intracortical; NOF >3 cm/extends toward medulla), explicitly described as a practical morphologic convention.
2. Plain-film pattern, growth-related migration/sclerosis and a representative user-supplied radiograph pair.
3. When plain films suffice; MRI as problem-solving for uncertainty/fracture concern; cautious fracture-risk and management framing.
4. Embedded pattern-retrieval question; three portable rules.

The header uses an original code-drawn schematic of eccentric metaphyseal lucency and a sclerotic rim; the case image is a crop from the provided PDF. No AI-generated diagnostic image or unverified external case is used.

## Scientific and implementation notes

- More-than-50% involvement appears in the PDF and published CT/radiographic fracture-risk work. It is presented as a concern cue only; neither lesion size nor that percentage alone dictates surgery.
- The Herget study is a retrospective cohort, so observed fracture association is not framed as universal risk prediction.
