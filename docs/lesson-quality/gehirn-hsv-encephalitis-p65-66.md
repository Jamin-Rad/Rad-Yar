# HSV encephalitis · Gehirn.pdf pp. 65–66

## Scope and proposed integration

- Topic: radiologic recognition of HSV encephalitis pattern and main mimics.
- Proposed exact curriculum entry: existing ID `herpes-enzephalitis`; link `/gehirn/infektionen/herpes-enzephalitis`; proposed status `inProgress`.
- Learning outcome: identify the typical asymmetric limbic/temporo-insular-inferofrontal distribution across sequences, describe important limitations and mimics, and recognize that suspected encephalitis requires urgent clinical management.
- Audience/prerequisite: radiology learners with basic brain MRI sequence literacy.
- This is an imaging lesson, not an infectious-disease diagnostic or treatment protocol. No global MCQ/cards were changed; one local embedded case question provides immediate feedback.

## Teaching path and media

1. Read the distribution before naming an organism.
2. Inspect supplied axial FLAIR case for temporal/insular signal and asymmetry.
3. Compare CT, T1/T2-FLAIR, DWI/ADC, SWI/T2* and post-contrast imaging.
4. Contrast the pattern with autoimmune/limbic disease, peri-ictal change and infiltrating tumour; apply a short clinically bounded retrieval question.
5. Compress to three reportable rules.

## Supporting source review

- Li S, Nguyen IP, Urbanczyk K. “Common infectious diseases of the central nervous system—clinical features and imaging characteristics.” *Quantitative Imaging in Medicine and Surgery*. 2020;10(12):2227–2259. DOI: 10.21037/qims-20-886. The HSV imaging section supports temporal/insular MRI pattern, variable diffusion/enhancement and limits of pattern attribution. Review article, not primary cohort.
- Lim & Thurnher. “Intracranial Infection and Inflammation.” *Diseases of the Brain, Head and Neck, Spine 2024–2027: Diagnostic Imaging*. NCBI Bookshelf, NBK608614, chapter 6. Supports asymmetric medial temporal/hippocampal/insular swelling and signal, typical basal ganglia sparing, and autoimmune mimic. Textbook chapter.
- Tunkel AR et al. IDSA “The Management of Encephalitis.” *Clinical Infectious Diseases*. 2008;47(3):303–327. DOI: 10.1086/589747, PMID 18582201. Used solely to verify the urgency of empiric acyclovir and that therapy should not wait for diagnostic test results. It is an older guideline; current local infectious disease protocol was not independently reviewed.
