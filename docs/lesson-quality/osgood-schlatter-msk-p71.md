## Lesson promise and design

After the lesson, the learner should be able to describe the tibial-tubercle traction apophysis, recognize the radiographic and MRI findings listed by the source, and avoid diagnosing from apophyseal fragmentation alone.

Learning goals:

1. Locate the involved apophysis and relate it to the patellar-ligament insertion.
2. Describe the source-listed lateral-radiograph findings without treating fragmentation as specific.
3. Describe the marrow, tendon-insertion and soft-tissue findings the source attributes to MRI.
4. Interpret the single source case cautiously and retrieve the normal-variant caveat in a self-check.

Chapter sequence: affected structure and mechanism → radiograph and normal variant → additional MRI signs → source case, self-check and concise take-home rules. The route stays imaging-focused. The PDF's treatment bullets are recorded below but not turned into individualized treatment advice or expanded recommendations.

## External scientific checks

- Kheiralla et al. “Imaging Review of Normal and Abnormal Skeletal Maturation.” *RadioGraphics*. 2022;42(1):E1–E22. [RSNA article](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/rg.210088), DOI 10.1148/rg.210088. States that tibial-tubercle fragmentation may occur in healthy asymptomatic individuals and recommends assessing marrow and soft tissues in context.
- Ladenhauf HN, Seitlinger G, Green DW. “Osgood-Schlatter disease: a 2020 update of a common knee condition in children.” *Curr Opin Pediatr*. 2020;32(1):107–112. [PubMed 31714260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31714260/), DOI 10.1097/MOP.0000000000000842. Notes that a sex difference may no longer be evident, qualifying the older source's “boys > girls” statement.
- Rosenberg ZS, Kawelblum M, Cheung YY, Beltran J, Lehman WB, Grant AD. “Osgood-Schlatter lesion: fracture or tendinitis? Scintigraphic, CT, and MR imaging features.” *Radiology*. 1992;185(3):853–858. [PubMed 1438775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1438775/), DOI 10.1148/radiology.185.3.1438775. Imaging series supports caution against equating an ossicle or suspected avulsion with ongoing symptoms.
- Ecklund. “Sports-Related Injuries of the Pediatric Musculoskeletal System.” NCBI Bookshelf 2021–2024. [Chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK570147/). Notes tubercle fragmentation may reflect normal developmental ossification; used for the normal-variant caution.
