# CAA story examinations

Updated 2026-10-17. The two earlier constructed cases and their unreferenced comparison images have been replaced by the requested Radiopaedia cases: RMH Neuropathology, rID 40475 (recurrent intracranial haemorrhage), and Yves Leonard Voss, rID 147140 (CAA-related inflammation). The single maintained dataset is `data/caaStoryCases.mjs`. Source images are unchanged and credited under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; individual image URLs, sequences and studies are recorded in `public/caa/exams/radiopaedia/provenance.json`.

Each story has six sequential four-option decisions: clinical presentation, initial imaging, additional assessment, diagnostic integration, subsequent action/evolution, and conclusion. A numbered timeline gives orientation without letting users skip future evidence. Imaging uses 442 original slices in nine source stacks: initial CT (35), GRE (22), follow-up CT/CTA (36), FLAIR (22), SWI minIP (80), SWI (80), DWI (22), ADC (23) and post-contrast T1 (122). The source viewer was traversed from first to last slice; ordered file identities, attribution and per-frame SHA-256 hashes are in `public/caa/exams/radiopaedia/stacks/provenance.json`.

`data/caaExamSeries.mjs` defines explicit sequence whitelists per stage instead of accumulating previously unlocked media. The first CT, MRI and deteriorating-patient CT are isolated to their relevant steps. CAA-ri starts with FLAIR; blood/diffusion sequences appear in the next step, and contrast T1 only at diagnostic integration. Pathology and documented treatment-response stages show no unrelated baseline stack. Only the active sequence's adjacent slices are prefetched. Wheel, arrow keys, touch swipes, a range slider and previous/next buttons navigate slices, with bounded indices and visible slice counts. The enlarged dialog shares the current slice with the inline viewer. Changing stage resets the viewer to the relevant sequence and representative slice. Source pixels are unchanged; no follow-up MRI has been invented.

In learning mode, committing an answer locks the four choices, reveals the correct answer and explanation, and enables the next step. Letters A–D carry the selected state without additional radio circles. Timed mode withholds teaching feedback until review. Sessions allow eight minutes per case, use a wall-clock deadline, retain committed answers at expiration and report remaining decisions as unanswered. Final review covers each decision and the four domains: observation, investigation, interpretation and diagnosis. Existing case selection, mixed sessions, account access and other topics retain their integration.

Medical review follows the two original cases, [Boston v2.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9389452/) and [Auriel et al., CAA-ri criteria](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26720093/). Cerebellar bleeding counts as neither lobar nor deep. The recurrent-haemorrhage source does not explicitly document every Boston exclusion, so no unsupported formal MRI category is assigned. Limited tissue confirms sampled vascular amyloid without being equated with the complete postmortem Boston definition. Amyloid deposits alone do not prove inflammation, and plaques do not establish clinical Alzheimer dementia. For CAA-ri, asymmetric oedema, lobar microbleeds, subacute symptoms and response to steroids support a clinical-radiological diagnosis; response is not histological proof. DWI negativity alone does not rule out all ischaemia. Treatment remains a specialist decision after assessment of mimics.

## Editorial walkthrough: evidence before answers

Both stories were reviewed from beginning to end in German, English and Persian. The haemorrhage story now has a neutral presenting-symptom title. Before an imaging-observation question, the narrative gives clinical context and the available sequence rather than supplying the finding to select. Detailed report findings and competing explanations are discussed after commitment. This preserves the distinction between recognising a finding and interpreting it in the following decision: prior images remain legitimate clinical evidence, while the teaching text does not announce the later diagnosis.

The numbered timeline labels only the current and completed decisions. Future steps display a neutral “Später / Later / بعداً” label, including for assistive technology, so an upcoming CT/MRI label cannot answer the current investigation-choice question. No future sequence name is put in a tooltip or accessible label.

Clinical history appears once in the current stage, including the patient's age and essential background. Stages without imaging use a centred single column instead of repeating the vignette in an image-column placeholder. The past-information disclosure starts only after the first decision; source/adaptation commentary remains in final review rather than cluttering the active case.

The SWI/DWI question now uses four alternative imaging descriptions rather than mixing observed findings with implausible investigation rules. Its feedback explains susceptibility foci and diffusion without calling the pattern inflammatory before diagnostic integration. The initial CT-choice feedback no longer names the complication tested next. MRI differential exclusions, measured haematoma sizes and follow-up mass effect are retained in the appropriate post-answer explanations.

Regression coverage includes the specific cross-step spoilers above in all three languages. Browser walkthroughs additionally check that future narratives/questions/explanations are absent, case history contains only prior stages, feedback appears after commitment in learning mode and is withheld in timed mode, and the correct stage whitelist is used for image requests. The editorial review checks meaning; these automated guards protect the identified failure modes rather than claiming general semantic spoiler detection.
